# Automotive Insurance Policy Coverage and Fraud Prevention Standards

## Section 1: Coverage Types and Fraud Exposure

### 1.1 Liability Coverage
Liability coverage pays for damage the policyholder causes to other vehicles or property. Fraud exposure under liability policies includes staged accidents where the insured intentionally causes a collision to generate a claim, inflated third-party damage claims, and phantom vehicle scenarios where the other vehicle does not exist.

### 1.2 Collision Coverage
Collision coverage pays for damage to the policyholder's own vehicle regardless of fault. Fraud exposure includes pre-existing damage claimed as new accident damage, intentional vehicle damage to collect a settlement, and inflated repair estimates from cooperating repair shops.

### 1.3 All Perils (Comprehensive) Coverage
All perils coverage provides the broadest protection including theft, vandalism, natural disasters, and collisions. Fraud exposure is highest under all perils policies because the wide range of covered events creates more opportunities for fabricated claims. False theft reports, staged vandalism, and arson to collect on underwater vehicle loans are common fraud patterns.

## Section 2: Deductible and Premium Fraud Indicators

### 2.1 Deductible Selection Patterns
Policyholders who select unusually high deductibles (e.g., $700 or above) while maintaining minimum coverage may be seeking lower premiums with no intention of filing legitimate small claims. When such policyholders file claims above the deductible amount, the claim should receive additional scrutiny.

### 2.2 Premium-to-Risk Ratio
When the annual premium paid by the policyholder is significantly lower than the expected claim amount, this represents a premium-to-risk imbalance. This pattern is common among policyholders who purchase minimum coverage shortly before filing a pre-planned fraudulent claim.

### 2.3 Policy Inception and Claim Timing
Claims filed within the first 90 days of policy inception are flagged as potential fraud. The shorter the gap between policy inception and the first claim, the higher the fraud risk. Claims filed within 7 days of policy inception should receive mandatory SIU review.

## Section 3: Claim Amount Validation

### 3.1 Vehicle Valuation Standards
All claims involving total loss or significant damage must be validated against current market values using industry-standard valuation tools (e.g., Kelley Blue Book, NADA Guides). The claimed vehicle value should align with the declared vehicle price range at the time of policy inception.

### 3.2 Repair Estimate Verification
Repair estimates exceeding 70% of the vehicle's market value should trigger a total loss evaluation. Multiple repair estimates should be obtained for claims above $10,000. Repair shop affiliations with the policyholder must be disclosed and independently verified.

### 3.3 Supplemental Claim Analysis
Claims with multiple supplemental filings (3 or more supplements) indicate potential cost inflation. Each supplemental claim should be independently reviewed. The pattern of supplemental filings relative to the original claim amount should be analyzed for anomalies.

## Section 4: Agent and Representative Standards

### 4.1 Agent Type Risk Assessment
External agents (independent agents) have historically been associated with higher fraud rates compared to internal (captive) agents. This is attributed to reduced oversight, performance-based compensation structures, and less frequent compliance audits. Claims filed through external agents should receive adjusted risk scoring.

### 4.2 Representative Monitoring
Claims representatives (adjusters) should be monitored for unusual patterns in their claim portfolios. A representative with a disproportionately high number of approved claims in the SIU-flagged category should be audited. Representative-to-fraud correlation analysis should be conducted quarterly.

### 4.3 Conflict of Interest
Any relationship between the policyholder and the assigned claims representative, repair shop, or medical provider must be disclosed. Undisclosed conflicts of interest constitute grounds for claim denial and potential referral to law enforcement.
