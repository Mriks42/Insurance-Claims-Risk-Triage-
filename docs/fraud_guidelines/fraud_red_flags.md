# Automotive Insurance Fraud Red Flags and Indicators

## Section 1: Common Fraud Indicators

### 1.1 Policyholder Behavior Indicators
Claims filed within the first 30 days of a new policy or immediately following a policy change are considered high-risk. A pattern of frequent address changes within 6 months to 1 year prior to a claim may indicate policyholder instability or intent to defraud. Policyholders who have filed more than 2 claims in the past 12 months should receive elevated scrutiny during the triage process.

### 1.2 Claim Timing Indicators
Claims filed on weekends or holidays warrant additional review, as these times are associated with reduced oversight and delayed police response. Claims where the accident date and claim filing date are within the same week suggest premeditation, as legitimate accident victims typically take longer to file claims. A mismatch between the day of the accident and the day the claim was filed on the same day of the week may indicate coordinated staging.

### 1.3 Documentation and Witness Absence
The absence of a police report for claims involving significant damage or injury is a primary red flag. When both a police report and witnesses are absent, the claim should be automatically escalated to SIU review. External agent-filed claims without police documentation have a historically higher fraud rate than internal agent-filed claims.

### 1.4 Financial Indicators
High-deductible policies paired with frequent claims suggest potential moral hazard. Claims on vehicles valued above $60,000 with liability-only coverage are unusual and should be examined for potential fraud. The deductible-to-vehicle-value ratio should be assessed — ratios below 1% may indicate intentional underinsurance.

## Section 2: Vehicle and Policy Type Risk Factors

### 2.1 Vehicle Category Risk
Sport vehicles have consistently shown elevated fraud rates compared to sedan and utility categories. The combination of sport vehicles with liability-only base policies represents the highest-risk policy configuration for fraud.

### 2.2 Policy Type Analysis
Liability-only policies exhibit the highest fraud correlation, followed by collision and all-perils policies. Sedan-collision policy types have shown moderate fraud risk. The base policy type (Liability, Collision, All Perils) should be considered in conjunction with the vehicle category for comprehensive risk assessment.

### 2.3 Vehicle Age Considerations
New vehicles (under 2 years) and older vehicles (over 7 years) present different fraud profiles. New vehicle claims may involve inflated repair costs, while older vehicle claims may involve total loss fraud where the insured value exceeds actual market value.

## Section 3: Demographic Risk Factors

### 3.1 Age-Based Risk
Policyholders aged 16-25 (young drivers) exhibit higher fraud rates. This is particularly pronounced when combined with sport vehicle categories and high deductible selections. The age-risk relationship is non-linear, with a secondary elevation among policyholders over 65.

### 3.2 Marital Status
Single policyholders file fraudulent claims at a higher rate than married policyholders. This demographic factor should be weighted in conjunction with other indicators rather than used in isolation.
