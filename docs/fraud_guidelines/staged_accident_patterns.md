# Staged Accident and Collision Fraud Patterns

## Section 1: Overview of Staged Accident Fraud

### 1.1 Definition and Scope
Staged accident fraud occurs when one or more parties deliberately cause or fabricate a vehicle collision to generate an insurance claim. Unlike opportunistic fraud where a policyholder exaggerates a legitimate claim, staged accidents are entirely premeditated. They represent the highest-severity fraud category in automotive insurance and are the primary driver of SIU escalations involving liability coverage.

Staged accidents are disproportionately associated with liability-only policies because the fraudster collects from the other party's insurer rather than their own, reducing the risk of policy cancellation. Claims where the policyholder is at fault, no police report was filed, and no independent witness is present form the classic staged accident profile.

### 1.2 Economic Impact
Staged accident fraud costs the U.S. insurance industry an estimated $20–40 billion annually. Individual staged accident claims typically range from $15,000 to $100,000 when medical costs, vehicle damage, and legal fees are included. The high per-claim cost makes staged accidents the single most important fraud type to detect at the triage stage.

## Section 2: Common Staged Accident Schemes

### 2.1 The Swoop and Squat
A fraudster pulls in front of the target vehicle and brakes suddenly, causing a rear-end collision. The target vehicle's driver is typically found at fault under standard traffic law. Indicators include: policyholder at fault, no police report, no witnesses, sport or utility vehicle category, and a claim filed within the same week as the accident.

### 2.2 The Drive Down
A fraudster waves the target vehicle into traffic and then deliberately collides with it, later denying they gave the signal. The target is again found at fault. This scheme frequently involves external agents who recruit participants and coordinate the claim filing. Look for: external agent involvement, policyholder at fault, no witness present, and recent address changes suggesting a transient fraud ring.

### 2.3 The Side Swipe
Two vehicles in adjacent lanes — one swerves into the other. The fraudster claims the legitimate driver caused the collision. Indicators include: weekend accident timing, no police report, sport vehicle category, and a claim filed on the same day of the week as the accident date.

### 2.4 Phantom Vehicle Schemes
A claim is filed for damage caused by a vehicle that does not exist or cannot be identified. These are exclusively liability claims. Key indicators: liability-only base policy, no police report, no witness, policyholder at fault, and a high deductible suggesting the policyholder is not concerned about their own vehicle damage.

### 2.5 Paper Accidents
No collision occurs at all — the claim is entirely fabricated. These are the hardest to detect but leave the clearest documentary gaps: no police report, no witness, same-day filing, and often a very early claim relative to policy inception date.

## Section 3: Indicators Specific to Staged Accidents

### 3.1 Fault and Documentation Pattern
The combination of policyholder at fault with no police report is the single strongest indicator of a staged accident. Legitimate at-fault accidents almost always result in a police report because the at-fault driver has an incentive to document the scene to protect themselves legally. When this documentation is absent, the claim warrants mandatory escalation.

### 3.2 Witness Absence
Staged accidents are carefully orchestrated to avoid independent witnesses. When both a police report and witnesses are absent simultaneously, the probability of fraud increases substantially. Claims with this dual absence should be automatically routed to SIU regardless of the computed risk score.

### 3.3 Timing Patterns
Staged accidents cluster on weekends and late evenings when police response is slower and fewer bystanders are present. Claims filed within the same week as the accident date — particularly on the same day of the week — suggest the filing was pre-planned rather than reactive. A gap of zero days between accident and claim is a mandatory SIU trigger.

### 3.4 Vehicle and Policy Configuration
Sport vehicles are overrepresented in staged accident fraud because they are associated with higher repair costs and more aggressive driving profiles that make collision scenarios plausible. Liability-only coverage on a sport vehicle is the highest-risk policy configuration in the dataset. All Perils coverage on older vehicles (over 7 years) is the second highest-risk configuration due to total loss fraud potential.

### 3.5 Agent and Network Indicators
External agents are statistically associated with higher staged accident rates due to reduced oversight and performance-based compensation. When an external agent files a claim that also lacks a police report and witness, the claim should be cross-referenced against other claims filed by the same agent for pattern detection. Fraud rings often use the same agent to file multiple staged accident claims within a short period.

### 3.6 Address Change Proximity
A recent address change (within 6 months to 1 year before the claim) combined with a staged accident profile suggests the policyholder may be part of a transient fraud ring that relocates to avoid detection. This combination — recent address change, no police report, policyholder at fault — is a three-factor trigger for mandatory SIU referral.

## Section 4: Investigation Procedures for Staged Accidents

### 4.1 Scene Reconstruction
SIU investigators should request an independent accident reconstruction analysis for all suspected staged accidents. Reconstruction can identify inconsistencies between the claimed collision dynamics and the actual vehicle damage patterns. Pre-existing damage is a common finding in staged accident investigations.

### 4.2 Medical Provider Cross-Reference
Staged accident rings frequently use the same medical providers to generate inflated injury claims. Investigators should cross-reference the medical providers listed in the claim against known fraud networks in the NICB database. Repeated use of the same provider across multiple unrelated claims is a strong indicator of organized fraud.

### 4.3 Social Network Analysis
When multiple claims share the same agent, address, vehicle make, or accident location within a 90-day window, a network analysis should be conducted. Staged accident fraud is rarely isolated — it typically involves coordinated rings filing multiple claims simultaneously.

### 4.4 Recorded Statement Protocol
All policyholders in suspected staged accident claims must provide a recorded statement before any payment is made. Inconsistencies between the recorded statement and the written claim, or between multiple statements from the same policyholder, are grounds for denial and potential referral to law enforcement.

### 4.5 Timeline Verification
Investigators must independently verify the accident timeline by obtaining traffic camera footage, cell tower records, or GPS data where available. A claim where the policyholder cannot provide a verifiable timeline of events on the accident date should be treated as a paper accident until proven otherwise.
